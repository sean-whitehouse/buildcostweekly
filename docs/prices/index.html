What this means for your bid

Direction: futures kept sliding. Lumber futures closed at $568 per thousand board feet on August 14 — down another 1.9% from where the last snapshot found them, 9.8% off on the month, and 6.8% below a year ago. That extends the breakdown we flagged last week rather than reversing it. On the cash side we are running with one eye this week: Madison's has not published a framing composite newer than the week ending August 7, so the $558 in the table above is the same reading you saw last snapshot, not a fresh one. We would rather say that plainly than pass a week-old number off as this week's.

Two things are worth knowing behind the numbers. First, the demand story hasn't changed: US housing construction activity is soft, and Trading Economics attributes the recent slide to tariff uncertainty plus weaker building demand. Second — and this is the near-term event to have on your radar — the Commerce Department's final determination in the current Canadian softwood review is expected in late August. The preliminary combined antidumping and countervailing rate came in at 24.83%, down from 35.16%, with a separate 10% Section 232 tariff stacked on top. A final number can land above or below the preliminary one, and the cash market is the side of the market that would feel it.

One honesty note on the table: the futures year-over-year figure improved from −11.3% to −6.8% even though the price fell. That is the comparison base moving, not prices rising — lumber was dropping through mid-August last year, so the year-ago benchmark is lower. Read the level and the month change; the year column is noisier than it looks.

What that means practically: the advice below is close to unchanged from last week, deliberately. Futures have moved; the number your yard quotes off has not been repriced in any data we can see. A falling futures chart is still not a discount you have been offered.

  • Bidding a job that starts within 30 days? Unchanged: bid off a current written yard quote. Your counter price is set by the cash market, and the most recent cash print was flat. Nothing in a week of futures weakness justifies shaving your framing number.
  • Bidding now for a start 60+ days out? With futures making new lows and the last composite print flat, paying a premium to lock a price is still a weak argument. But a start that far out now straddles the late-August duty determination, which can cut either way. That is a good reason to ask your yard for a free written hold that spans it — the ask costs nothing, and it takes the outcome off your plate either way.
  • Buying for a job already under contract? Still no case for buying early on price. The trend that would have justified stockpiling has been gone for a month. If you have a specific delivery you cannot afford to have slip in late August, that is a scheduling reason to buy ahead — not a price one, and worth being honest with yourself about which it is.

Note on consistency: last snapshot called the futures/cash split the whole story and said not to bid a decline that hadn't reached your yard. A week later futures are lower and the cash side has no new print, so that call stands. If the next composite reading comes in below $558, the guidance above changes — that would be the first real evidence of relief reaching the counter.

FAQ

How often are these prices updated?

Every week. The date at the top of the page ("Week of...") is the week this snapshot covers.

Where do these numbers come from?

Two sources, both linked in the Sources section above: Trading Economics for lumber futures, and the NAHB/Madison's Lumber Price Index for the framing lumber composite. Both are dated so you can check the primary source yourself.

Why do the futures price and the composite price sometimes move in different directions?

Futures are traders' forward bet on where lumber is headed; the Madison's composite tracks what's actually trading in the cash market right now. They usually move together but can diverge for weeks at a time — when they disagree, the "What this means for your bid" callout says so.

Will my local lumberyard price match this exactly?

No. These are wholesale indexes, not retail prices — your yard's counter price will differ and typically lags these moves by 2–6 weeks. Always confirm with a written supplier quote before you bid; don't bid off the index number.

What materials besides lumber are tracked?

Right now, just framing lumber (futures and the Madison's composite). OSB/panel, drywall, rebar/steel, and ready-mix concrete are being added as reliable free sources are verified — see the note above if there's one you want prioritized.
