What this means for your bid

Direction: down for a third straight week, and the decline is picking up speed rather than fading. The framing composite — the cash index your lumberyard's counter price actually tracks — came in at $521.35 per thousand board feet for the week ending August 28, down about $9 (1.6%) on the week and down $37 (6.6%) from $558 a month ago. Lumber futures sat at $563.50 on September 2, roughly 2.0% below the $575 in the last snapshot and 6.2% under a month ago — their lowest area since April, per Trading Economics.

Three prints, one direction. This page called the first decline a data point and the second a reason to pick up the phone. The third does not turn it into a forecast, but it does make the month figure the number worth reading: a 6.6% drop on the cash index over four weeks is not noise, and it follows nearly three months of consistent increases. The market that ran up through the spring has given a meaningful piece of it back.

Two forces, pulling opposite ways. On the demand side, the Census Bureau's most recent housing starts report (July, released in August — the August figures are not out yet) put starts at a 1,239,000 annual rate, down 13.5% from a year ago, with single-family down 9.9%. Fewer houses framed means less lumber bought. On the supply side, mills have been cutting output: more than 20 sawmills in British Columbia have closed since 2023, and further curtailments are expected while consumption stays weak. That combination is why forecasts disagree so loudly right now — weak demand pushes prices down, while shrinking capacity props them up. This page is not going to pretend to know which wins. What it can tell you is what has actually printed, and what has printed is three weeks lower.

The standing caution has not changed. The cash index is wholesale. Your yard's counter price typically lags these moves by two to six weeks, and a yard sitting on inventory bought at $558 has no obligation to hand the difference back. Three weeks of decline is a reason to ask, not a discount you already have.

Still on the calendar: the Commerce Department's final results in the seventh administrative review of Canadian softwood lumber duties. The post-preliminary results issued June 30 pointed to a combined antidumping and countervailing rate of 24.83%, down from the 35.16% currently in effect — but cash deposit rates do not change until the final results are published, and as of today they have not been. Reporting now points to October rather than the August timing noted here previously. A final number can land above or below the preliminary one, and the cash market is the side that would feel it.

Why the year column is gone. This table used to carry a year-over-year figure. It has been removed, and it is worth saying why rather than letting it quietly disappear. This week the sources stopped agreeing with each other: NAHB published the composite at +9.3% versus a year ago, while Madison's own weekly index put the comparable 2025 week at $536 — which against today's $521.35 is roughly 3% lower, not 9% higher. Futures had the same problem (NAHB +2.9% for August 28, Trading Economics +7.5% for September 2). A number we cannot reconcile is a number you should not be bidding off, so it is off the page until it stabilises. The level, the week, and the month figures are unaffected and come straight from the sources below. If the year comparison becomes reliable again, it comes back and this note goes away.

What that means practically: the guidance below is unchanged in direction from last week and stronger in degree. Re-price rather than assume your last quote still holds.

  • Bidding a job that starts within 30 days? Get a fresh written quote before you bid — this is now the third week running that the number on an older quote was set in a different market. The cash index is down 6.6% on the month. That is not the same as assuming you will get 6.6% back at the counter; it is a reason to make the call rather than reuse a stale number, and then to bid what the new quote says.
  • Bidding now for a start 60+ days out? Paying a premium to lock a price is still the weaker argument — you generally do not pay up to lock a market that has moved your way three weeks running. But the duty final is now expected around October and mill curtailments are a live supply story, so consider asking your yard for a free written hold that spans your start date. The ask costs nothing and takes the question off your plate either way.
  • Buying for a job already under contract? Still no price case for buying early — the recent direction has been down. If you have a delivery you genuinely cannot let slip, buying ahead is a scheduling decision. Make it on those grounds and be honest with yourself that it is not a price call.

Note on consistency: this snapshot is being published Thursday rather than Monday — the weekly run was late, and the data below is the most recent available as of September 3, not a week of catching up. The bid guidance has now held for three snapshots in a row: re-quote before you bid. If the composite prints flat or higher next week, this page will say so plainly instead of quietly dropping the subject. What does not change either way is the underlying rule — you bid off the written quote in your hand, not off this table.

FAQ

How often are these prices updated?

Every week. The date at the top of the page ("Week of...") is the week this snapshot covers.

Where do these numbers come from?

Two sources, both linked in the Sources section above: Trading Economics for lumber futures, and the NAHB/Madison's Lumber Price Index for the framing lumber composite. Both are dated so you can check the primary source yourself.

Why do the futures price and the composite price sometimes move in different directions?

Futures are traders' forward bet on where lumber is headed; the Madison's composite tracks what's actually trading in the cash market right now. They usually move together but can diverge for weeks at a time — when they disagree, the "What this means for your bid" callout says so.

Will my local lumberyard price match this exactly?

No. These are wholesale indexes, not retail prices — your yard's counter price will differ and typically lags these moves by 2–6 weeks. Always confirm with a written supplier quote before you bid; don't bid off the index number.

What materials besides lumber are tracked?

Right now, just framing lumber (futures and the Madison's composite). OSB/panel, drywall, rebar/steel, and ready-mix concrete are being added as reliable free sources are verified — see the note above if there's one you want prioritized.
