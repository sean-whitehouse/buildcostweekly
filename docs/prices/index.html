What this means for your bid

Direction: the two markets have split again — and this time it's the futures market that moved. Lumber futures broke down hard: $579 per thousand board feet, about 8.8% below the July 20 snapshot, 7.7% off on the month, and 11.3% below a year ago. That's a three-month low, and it undoes the rebound we flagged in the last snapshot. The cash market — the Madison's framing composite, which is closer to what your yard actually buys from — has not followed it down. The composite sits at $558, unchanged for two straight weeks after a +2.0% run over the past month. Its year-over-year figure has flipped negative (−1.6%) for the first time in a while, but flat is not falling.

The reason for the split is the plainest part of the story. Futures are pricing in weaker demand — US housing demand has softened and mortgage rates recently hit their highest level in a year. The cash market is being held up by cost: sawmill capacity is still constrained, and the duty picture on Canadian softwood remains unsettled, with a US Commerce determination in the current administrative review expected around now. Traders can sell a demand outlook overnight; a mill's delivered cost doesn't drop that fast.

What that means practically: don't bid a price decline that hasn't reached your yard yet. Futures turning down is an early signal, not a discount you can spend. When the cash composite starts printing lower — not flat, lower — that's when relief is real.

  • Bidding a job that starts within 30 days? Unchanged: bid off a current written yard quote. Your counter price is set by the cash market, and the cash market is flat, not falling. Nothing here justifies shaving your framing number.
  • Bidding now for a start 60+ days out? This is where the news helps you a little. With futures at a three-month low and the composite no longer climbing, the case for paying up to protect a price is weaker than it was three weeks ago. A free written hold from your yard is still worth asking for, but a wide framing contingency is harder to justify in a market that has stopped rising. If a customer is pushing on price, this is the softest framing market since spring.
  • Buying for a job already under contract? No case for buying early right now. Every reason to buy ahead of schedule — a rising trend, a breakout, a tightening cash market — has weakened. If your storage plan was based on the July run-up, it's reasonable to go back to buying on your normal schedule.

Note on consistency: the last snapshot called framing "firm" and said the relief case had weakened. Futures have since reversed that. The cash market has not — so the bidding advice above changes less than the futures number alone would suggest. That gap is the whole point of tracking both.

FAQ

How often are these prices updated?

Every week. The date at the top of the page ("Week of...") is the week this snapshot covers.

Where do these numbers come from?

Two sources, both linked in the Sources section above: Trading Economics for lumber futures, and the NAHB/Madison's Lumber Price Index for the framing lumber composite. Both are dated so you can check the primary source yourself.

Why do the futures price and the composite price sometimes move in different directions?

Futures are traders' forward bet on where lumber is headed; the Madison's composite tracks what's actually trading in the cash market right now. They usually move together but can diverge for weeks at a time — when they disagree, the "What this means for your bid" callout says so.

Will my local lumberyard price match this exactly?

No. These are wholesale indexes, not retail prices — your yard's counter price will differ and typically lags these moves by 2–6 weeks. Always confirm with a written supplier quote before you bid; don't bid off the index number.

What materials besides lumber are tracked?

Right now, just framing lumber (futures and the Madison's composite). OSB/panel, drywall, rebar/steel, and ready-mix concrete are being added as reliable free sources are verified — see the note above if there's one you want prioritized.
